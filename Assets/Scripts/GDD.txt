# Fish the Fisher

## Game Design & Technical Document

**Version:** 2.0
**Last Updated:** July 2026

---

# Project Overview

Fish the Fisher is a turn-based deckbuilding roguelite centered around strategic fishing instead of combat.

Rather than fighting enemies, the player manipulates the lake, reveals encounters, manages delayed effects, builds Fishing Strength, and catches increasingly valuable fish.

The game aims to combine the long-term planning of Slay the Spire with the relaxing theme of fishing while creating its own unique identity.

The project is being developed in Unity using URP.

---

# Core Design Philosophy

The game should reward planning over reaction.

Most mechanics should be deterministic.

The player should feel clever because they prepared several turns ahead rather than because they got lucky.

Information should be visible whenever possible.

The UI should communicate game state instead of forcing the player to memorize it.

Complexity should emerge from combinations between simple cards.

---

# Gameplay Loop

During an encounter:

1. Reveal encounter cards.
2. Fish are added to the queue.
3. The first queued fish becomes Active.
4. Player spends Focus to play cards.
5. Cards manipulate:

   * Fishing Strength
   * Focus
   * Encounter Deck
   * Player Deck
   * Delayed Effects
   * Fish
6. End Turn.
7. Delayed effects activate.
8. Fish durations decrease.
9. Player catches fish before they escape.

---

# Core Resources

## Focus

Primary resource.

Used for playing cards.

Resets every turn.

Can increase Maximum Focus.

Current Focus and Maximum Focus are tracked separately.

---

## Fishing Strength

Represents the player's current ability to catch fish.

Compared against the Active Fish's required strength.

Many cards temporarily modify Fishing Strength.

---

# Fish

Fish replace enemies.

Every fish contains:

* Name
* Sprite
* Required Fishing Strength
* Duration
* Reward
* Passive Ability (optional)

Fish are objectives rather than opponents.

---

# Fish Passives

Some fish may have passive abilities that modify the encounter while they remain active.

Examples:

* Cannot have its duration increased.
* Fishing Strength bonuses are halved.
* Revealing encounters costs 1 Focus.
* Gains +1 Required Strength each turn.
* When caught, reveal another encounter.
* Cannot be caught on the first turn.
* Escaping removes a random card from your hand.
* Playing Cycle cards grants this fish +1 Strength.

Passives should be simple but create interesting situations.

Not every fish should have one.

---

# Fish Queue

Only one fish is Active.

Additional revealed fish remain queued.

When the current fish leaves or is caught:

The next queued fish automatically becomes Active.

Queued fish remain visible in the Encounter Reveal Area until promoted.

# Delayed Cards

Cards may activate immediately or after one or more turns.

Cards with multiple delayed activations only display the FIRST delay.

Example:

Delay Icon:

⏳1

Description:

Reveal 1 card, then 2 cards, then 3 cards from the encounter deck separately, each on consecutive turns.

The detailed schedule is shown after the card is played inside the Effects Panel.

# Rod System

Rods are intended to become one of the game's defining mechanics.

Players may eventually own multiple rods.

Only one rod is equipped during an encounter.

Rods function similarly to passive loadouts.

Examples:

Increase starting Focus.

Fish remain longer.

Increase Fishing Strength.

Reveal additional encounters.

Modify delayed effects.

Rod design should encourage different playstyles rather than simply providing stat increases.

# Code Philosophy

Separate gameplay from visuals.

Animation should never determine gameplay.

Managers should remain specialized.

Favor ScriptableObjects for reusable gameplay logic.

Keep systems modular and easy to extend.

Avoid giant manager classes.

---

# Long-Term Vision

Fish the Fisher should feel like a highly replayable strategy game where every fishing encounter becomes a puzzle.

The game should encourage planning several turns ahead through delayed effects, card combinations, rod selection, fish passives, and deckbuilding decisions.

Players should constantly ask themselves:

"How can I manipulate the lake to catch this fish most efficiently?"

rather than

"How much damage can I deal this turn?"

Fishing should be the core mechanic—not merely a theme placed over a traditional combat system.

The ultimate goal is for Fish the Fisher to establish its own identity within the deckbuilder genre rather than simply being "Slay the Spire with fish."
